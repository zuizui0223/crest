# Manuscript-facing insert — empirical anchors for CREST

> Status: candidate main-text insertion. These examples ground the world → state → law argument; they do not serve as proof of the finite CREST theorems.

## Candidate subsection: Ecological anchors from island and urban systems

Two empirical systems illustrate why the CREST distinction between a visible present descriptor and a response-preserving state matters.

**Island interaction replacement in Mauritius.** A reconstruction of Mauritian plant–frugivore interactions found that losses of native interaction partnerships after extinction were accompanied by a similar number of novel interactions involving introduced species. At the level of binary network topology, this could look like functional replacement. Yet seed-handling behavior showed that many former seed-dispersal interactions had been replaced by seed predation, making restoration of the original dispersal function unlikely (Heinen et al., 2023). The relevant response therefore does not factor through a quotient that records only whether a plant–frugivore link exists. An earlier in-situ restoration experiment on Île aux Aigrettes makes the complementary point. After loss of native large-bodied frugivores had left the endemic ebony *Diospyros egrettarum* seed-dispersal limited, introduced Aldabra giant tortoises consumed and dispersed its fruits; gut passage improved germination and new seedlings established widely (Griffiths et al., 2011). In CREST terms, the useful state distinction is not simply `interaction absent/present` but whatever interaction properties preserve the response assigned to the state. A newly available restoration action can therefore make distinctions in interaction identity and function scientifically consequential.

**Adaptive law portability across cities.** The Global Urban Evolution Project sampled 110,019 white-clover plants from 6,169 populations across 160 cities. Urban–rural gradients were associated with significant cyanogenesis clines in only 47% of cities, and the strength of those clines varied with city-specific changes in drought stress and vegetation cover; genomic analyses supported adaptive evolution but also showed that the degree of parallel adaptation differed among cities (Santangelo et al., 2022). Earlier four-city work likewise found an urban decline in cyanogenesis in Toronto, New York, and Boston but not Montreal, with winter ground temperature and snow cover implicated in the difference. The lesson is not that urban evolutionary rules are arbitrary. It is that `urban` is often too coarse a state for a portable fitness-response law. If the relevant response depends on drought, vegetation, freezing regime, and their local covariance, a quotient that collapses those distinctions need not support one city-independent rule. This is the ecological counterpart of domain-relative law validity: the same nominal state label can remain descriptively convenient after it ceases to preserve the response required for transfer.

These examples also show why islands and cities form a useful matched pair for CREST. Oceanic islands make historical loss, colonization, and interaction replacement unusually visible; cities make intervention, connectivity, and rapidly changing selective context unusually visible. They are not the same ecological system. They are two different ways in which a present label can erase distinctions that become consequential under a changed future or response contract.

## Optional supporting box

### Two further discriminating systems

**Izu Islands *Campanula*.** Inoue and Amano (1986) documented a mainland-to-island change from large bumblebee-dominated pollination toward smaller halictid pollinators, smaller flowers, and increased self-compatibility; Inoue (1988) found Oshima to be an important exception because bumblebees persisted there and most plants remained self-incompatible. Thus an `island population` partition is already too coarse for the reproductive response. The system is especially useful prospectively: if populations with similar present floral descriptors respond differently when a large-pollinator interaction becomes available, the present snapshot fails the CREST sufficiency test directly.

**Urban pollinator connectivity.** Pollen-tracking among isolated urban green patches has shown realized pollen movement even without formal green corridors, so geometric isolation does not by itself certify functional closure. More directly, Li, Clements and Memmott (2026) constructed 30-m artificial linear features linking phytometer assemblages and found higher overall seed set in linked treatments, with strong benefits for several bee-pollinated plants but weaker effects for fly- and moth-pollinated plants. A connection therefore changes the future-response repertoire, but not through one mechanism-independent law. The adequate state must preserve at least the pollinator-guild or interaction distinctions that modify the response to connection.

## Claim firewall

Do not write that these examples empirically prove CREST. Safe wording is:

- they instantiate failures predicted by the CREST distinction between a coarse present descriptor and a response-preserving state;
- they show that biologically natural candidate quotients can fail to preserve a declared response;
- they motivate discriminating empirical tests of quotient adequacy;
- the finite mathematical theorems remain independent of these cases.
