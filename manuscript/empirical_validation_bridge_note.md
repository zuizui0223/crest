# Empirical validation bridge for the Biology & Philosophy manuscript

## Recommended insertion after the quotient-law / representational-stability discussion

The CREST distinction between a coarse state label and a portable response law has direct ecological analogues. In the Global Urban Evolution Project, white-clover cyanogenesis was measured across 160 cities. City-specific urban-rural clines varied in sign and strength: for example, standardized-distance coefficients were negative in Albuquerque, Beijing, Kyoto, Lisbon and Uppsala, but positive in Amsterdam, Antwerp, New York and Toronto. Thus `urbanization` alone does not support one signed cyanogenesis law across cities. The public analysis pipeline further predicts city cline strength from environmental context, and its repeated elastic-net fits retain combinations of environmental change and environmental background with high frequency, including annual potential-evapotranspiration change × mean summer vegetation (100/100 fits) and several snow/vegetation/aridity terms (>90/100 fits). The held-out predictive signal is positive but modest, so the result should not be read as identifying one final ecological state. It instead illustrates the CREST sequence: a coarse quotient is falsified, a richer quotient recovers some portable signal, and substantial unresolved state variation remains.

A complementary island example occurs in Mauritius. Reconstruction of plant-frugivore networks shows that replacement of lost binary interactions by novel interactions does not generally restore seed-dispersal function, because seed dispersal can be replaced by seed predation. Conversely, the deliberate introduction of Aldabra giant tortoises on Île aux Aigrettes restored a missing large-frugivore interaction for *Diospyros egrettarum*, improving germination and seedling recruitment. Hence interaction topology alone is too coarse a state for the functional response of interest; the relevant quotient must retain distinctions such as seed handling and replacement identity when those distinctions change future demographic response.

These examples are ecological anchors, not proofs of the finite CREST theorems. Their role is to show that the distinctions formalized by CREST correspond to empirically consequential failures of coarse ecological labels.
