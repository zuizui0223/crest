# When Ecological Responsibilities Interact
## Non-additive State Debt in Conservation Representation

**Target:** The American Naturalist — Major Article

## Abstract

Ecological state variables compress physically different systems into classes treated as equivalent for prediction, intervention, comparison, or reporting. The required resolution of such a state can change when scientific responsibilities are combined. We develop Contract-Relative Ecological State Theory (CREST) around this joint-responsibility problem. On one declared finite ecological common lift, each responsibility induces a refinement closure of a baseline state partition. We define the individual state debt of responsibility i as \(D_i=\log_2|C_i(B)|-\log_2|B|\), the debt of satisfying all responsibilities jointly as \(D_{\rm joint}=\log_2|J|-\log_2|B|\), and the non-additive interaction term \(\Delta=D_{\rm joint}-\sum_iD_i\). A marked-cycle family gives \(D_1=1\), \(D_2=0\), and \(D_{\rm joint}=\log_2 n\), so \(\Delta=\log_2 n-1\) and is unbounded. Thus separately budgeting monitoring or state resolution for future-response, inherited-semantic, mechanistic, and evidential responsibilities can underestimate the resolution required when those responsibilities are enforced together. The converse boundary is sharp: if every individual responsibility leaves the baseline unchanged, then the baseline is already a common fixed point and \(D_{\rm joint}=\Delta=0\). A before/after obstruction-spectrum comparison further shows that joint burden can increase even when every standalone debt is unchanged, with the increase decomposable into pairwise and higher-order interaction terms. Supporting results show that carrier gain alone does not bound state burden and that sequential response depth bounds one important source of debt by \(H\log_2r\). CREST therefore treats ecological state not as a fixed snapshot but as the least-information quotient licensed for a declared bundle of scientific responsibilities.

**Keywords:** ecological state; conservation monitoring; coarse graining; scientific representation; counterfactual response; state complexity; model adequacy

## 1. Ecological state is a responsibility-bearing compression

Ecological science routinely uses state descriptions that are much coarser than the underlying world. A lake is called turbid, a population persistent, a community functionally intact, or a restoration site recoverable even though many physical and historical differences remain within each label. Such compression is not a defect. Scientific state variables are useful because they erase differences that do not matter for the work assigned to them.

The difficulty is that the work assigned to a state is rarely singular. One classification may be expected to support future interventions, remain interpretable after structural replacement, remain valid across retained mechanism alternatives, and be identifiable from a monitoring system. Each responsibility can be defensible in isolation. Their conjunction need not be.

CREST starts from a set \(\Omega\) of admissible ecological worlds, represented schematically as temporally extended objects \(\omega=(h_t,x_t,\mathcal F_t)\), where history, present configuration, and future-response structure may all matter. A scientific contract declares which distinctions the state must preserve. In the current finite theory these obligations are represented on one common carrier by refinement closures

\[
C_1,\ldots,C_k
\]

acting above a baseline partition \(B\). The adequate joint state is the least common fixed point

\[
J=(C_1\vee\cdots\vee C_k)(B).
\]

This fixed-point substrate is classical. The ecological question is what happens when separately reasonable responsibilities interact on the same state description.

The central claim of this paper is therefore not merely that richer tasks require richer states. It is that **the information burden of a bundle of ecological responsibilities is not generally the sum of the burdens measured one responsibility at a time**.

## 2. Individual and joint state debt

For a finite partition \(P\), write its state information relative to baseline \(B\) as the logarithmic increase in class count. For responsibility \(i\), define

\[
D_i=\log_2|C_i(B)|-\log_2|B|.
\]

This is the debt visible when responsibility \(i\) is applied alone to the baseline. Define the debt of satisfying every declared responsibility together as

\[
D_{\rm joint}=\log_2|J|-\log_2|B|.
\]

The interaction term is

\[
\boxed{\Delta=D_{\rm joint}-\sum_iD_i.}
\]

A positive \(\Delta\) means that responsibility-by-responsibility accounting misses distinctions generated by interaction among the closures. An audit that appears cheap in isolation can become expensive after another audit has created distinctions on which it can act.

This quantity belongs to the synthesis layer. A future-sufficiency theory can define its own required partition, a semantic-transport theory can define a carried repair, a mechanism-robustness theory can define a candidate-safe quotient, and an evidence theory can define what is licensed to report. None of those component problems, taken alone, defines \(D_{\rm joint}\) across the full responsibility bundle.

### Theorem 1 — unbounded non-additive joint debt

For every integer \(n\ge2\), there exists a finite CREST contract with two audit closures on an \(n\)-world baseline such that

\[
D_1=1,
\qquad
D_2=0,
\qquad
D_{\rm joint}=\log_2 n.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\Delta=\log_2 n-1,}
\]

which is unbounded as \(n\to\infty\).

The witness is a marked directed cycle. One audit introduces a single marked distinction. The second audit does nothing to the unmarked baseline when considered alone, so its individual debt is zero. Once the mark exists, however, successor consistency propagates that distinction around the cycle until the common fixed point is discrete. The joint system therefore requires \(n\) classes even though the separate accounting sees only the initial one-bit split.

The mechanism is simple on purpose. The point is not that a one-point-marked cycle is a historically new automata construction; it is not. The result isolates the precise accounting failure relevant to the ecological synthesis: **one scientific responsibility can create the distinctions that make another responsibility informative**.

### Conservation interpretation

Suppose a monitoring programme is budgeted by treating responsibilities independently. Future-response adequacy is assigned one set of measurements, semantic continuity another, mechanism robustness another, and evidence licensing a fourth. The natural additive estimate is

\[
\sum_iD_i.
\]

The theorem shows that no universal guarantee makes this estimate sufficient. The jointly adequate state can require arbitrarily more resolution than the sum of the debts exposed from the original baseline.

This matters because monitoring variables are often selected against already defined categories. CREST reverses that order. If satisfying one responsibility changes the state partition, the obstruction set seen by another responsibility can also change. Monitoring design should therefore be checked against the final common state rather than against independent audit outputs alone.

The interpretation is not that every real conservation programme will exhibit large synergy, nor that \(\Delta\) is automatically positive. It is a structural warning about modular accounting. A collection of individually adequate state descriptions need not compose into an adequate joint state description at the same total information cost.

### Theorem 2 — zero individual debt cannot hide joint debt

The interaction is not completely unconstrained. If

\[
D_i=0
\quad\text{for every }i,
\]

then each audit leaves the baseline partition unchanged. The baseline is therefore already fixed by every closure and hence is their common fixed point. Consequently

\[
\boxed{D_{\rm joint}=0,\qquad\Delta=0.}
\]

This lemma gives the useful boundary. Hidden joint debt requires at least one responsibility to create a real distinction somewhere. Purely dormant audits cannot bootstrap one another from an unchanged baseline.

### Quantitative corollary — representational change can be interaction-only

The static joint-debt result can be compared across two declared contracts on the same named responsibility set. Write before and after quantities with superscripts \(-\) and \(+\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta D_{\rm joint}
=
D_{\rm joint}^+-D_{\rm joint}^-
=
\sum_i\delta D_i+\delta\Delta.
}
\]

If every standalone debt is unchanged,

\[
\delta D_i=0\quad\text{for every }i,
\]

then any nonzero change in joint burden is entirely interaction-generated:

\[
\boxed{
\delta D_{\rm joint}=\delta\Delta.
}
\]

The finite obstruction spectrum makes that interaction anatomically explicit. Let \(m(S)\) be the Möbius/Harsanyi dividend of responsibility coalition \(S\). For interaction order \(k\), define

\[
M_k=\sum_{|S|=k}\delta m(S).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta D_{\rm joint}=\sum_{k\ge1}M_k,
\qquad
M_1=\sum_i\delta D_i,
\qquad
\delta\Delta=\sum_{k\ge2}M_k.
}
\]

In a six-world CCOC/MLTR/MRM comparison, all three standalone debts are unchanged, but switching on the cross-responsibility activation chain changes the required joint state from three to five classes. Joint debt rises from

\[
0.5849625007
\quad\text{to}\quad
1.3219280949\ \text{bits},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\delta D_{\rm joint}=+0.7369655942\ \text{bit}.}
\]

The entire increase is interaction-generated and decomposes as

\[
+0.7369655942
=
\underbrace{+0.4150374993}_{\text{CCOC × MLTR}}
+
\underbrace{+0.3219280949}_{\text{CCOC × MLTR × MRM}}
\quad\text{bits}.
\]

Thus the change is `interaction-only`: the direct component is unchanged, pairwise and three-way coupling both increase, and the pairwise term is larger in absolute magnitude. Reversing the contract change produces an interaction-only decrease with the same active interaction orders and opposite signs.

This comparison matters because a monitoring programme can appear stable when every responsibility is re-evaluated separately. The state burden can nevertheless change because the responsibilities have become more or less coupled. Representational stability is therefore a property of the joint obstruction structure, not only of the marginal responsibility scores.

## 3. Where the interacting responsibilities come from

CREST does not treat all sources of refinement as one theorem. The component theories identify distinct ecological reasons that a present merge can fail.

**Future/composition responsibility.** CCOC asks whether states that are equivalent under a restricted future remain equivalent when new interactions, connections, or interventions become addressable. Its sharp bounded-query constructions show that bounded local arity alone does not bound the state gap when response depth can grow.

**Inherited-semantic responsibility.** MLTR asks whether a classification carried from a source system remains well defined and decision-sufficient after structural replacement. Its distinctive result is route coherence and the necessary-and-sufficient historical context required when different replacement routes carry different terminal meanings.

**Mechanism responsibility.** MRM asks when several retained mechanisms may safely remain unresolved because they agree on required predictions, and when active interventions are necessary to collapse the compatible mechanism set. Its canonical frontier quantifies exact identification burden; cost-aware dynamic programming is an operational extension rather than the novelty anchor.

**Evidence responsibility.** CED is downstream. Once a scientific distinction is required, evidence may or may not identify it. CREST therefore separates required state, identified state, and reportable target.

The point of the joint-debt quantity is not to erase these boundaries. It is to ask what happens after their obligations are placed on one declared ecological carrier.

## 4. Supporting quantitative structure

The joint-debt result changes the paper's headline but does not make the earlier quantitative results irrelevant.

First, capability gain alone does not bound state burden. A connected finite controlled family can add one viable world while forcing an arbitrarily large refinement of a retained present slice. Thus a small increase in what managers can do need not imply a small increase in what they must distinguish.

Second, the missing resource in that construction can be made explicit. Along one sequential response channel of depth \(H\), with at most \(r_h\) distinguishable retained outcomes at stage \(h\), the number of distinguishable response signatures is bounded by

\[
\prod_{h=1}^{H}r_h,
\]

so the additional state information is bounded by

\[
\Delta K\le\sum_{h=1}^{H}\log_2r_h.
\]

For the homogeneous case,

\[
\boxed{\Delta K\le H\log_2r.}
\]

A connected CREST family attains equality. This result explains one way individual debt can become large: response depth creates distinguishable counterfactual coordinates. The new \(\Delta\) result addresses a different question. Even after each responsibility's individual burden has been measured, **their interaction can create additional debt not visible in those separate measurements**.

The two statements therefore occupy different levels:

\[
\text{response capacity bounds debt within one responsibility,}
\]

whereas

\[
\text{joint debt measures interaction across responsibilities.}
\]

The before/after obstruction spectrum adds a third level: it determines whether a change in joint burden is attributable to changed standalone responsibilities, changed cross-responsibility interaction, or both.

## 5. Ecological prediction and monitoring consequence

The framework suggests a concrete empirical programme. Systems should be especially vulnerable to positive joint debt when scientific responsibilities are coupled by the distinctions they expose. Examples include restoration problems where intervention response reveals latent mechanism, structural change alters the meaning of inherited categories, and the newly required categories are not resolved by routine monitoring.

This yields a stronger prediction than “complex ecosystems need more monitoring.” The relevant signal is **activation across responsibilities**. If a distinction introduced for one task changes which pairs must be separated for another task, independent monitoring budgets will be systematically optimistic.

Operationally, a CREST analysis should therefore proceed in the following order: specify the common ecological world set and baseline state; apply all declared responsibilities to their common fixed point; measure \(D_{\rm joint}\) and compare it with the individual debts; when the contract changes, compare the full obstruction spectra and diagnose direct versus interaction-generated change; only then design or evaluate the evidence system against the final required state. A positive \(\Delta\) is a warning that modular monitoring design misses interaction-generated distinctions, while an interaction-only increase shows that this deficit can worsen without any standalone responsibility score increasing.

The marked-cycle family is an extremal proof, not an ecological frequency claim. Real applications may have \(\Delta<0\), \(\Delta=0\), or \(\Delta>0\). Negative values would indicate overlap or redundancy among responsibilities; zero would indicate additive accounting at the chosen baseline; positive values indicate synergistic refinement. Likewise, before/after changes may be direct-only, interaction-only, mixed, or null. Estimating the distribution and magnitude of these regimes in empirical ecological models is a separate task.

## 6. Novelty boundary

CREST does not claim novelty for closure operators, partition lattices, bisimulation-style refinement, marked cycles, automata minimization, Shapley values, Möbius inversion, Harsanyi dividends, or the elementary logarithmic accounting used above. Those are mathematical substrate.

The contribution claimed here is narrower and ecological: once future response, inherited semantics, retained mechanisms, and evidence obligations are treated as distinct scientific responsibilities on a common ecological world set, their state-resolution burdens need not add. The interaction term \(\Delta\) makes that failure explicit, while the obstruction spectrum makes its direct, pairwise, and higher-order anatomy measurable and its before/after change diagnosable inside the CREST architecture.

This boundary also protects the companion papers. CCOC remains a future-interface theory, MLTR a carried-semantics theory, MRM a mechanism-ambiguity and active-resolution theory, and CED an evidence-licensing theory. The synthesis uses them as responsibility classes without absorbing their strongest standalone results.

## 7. Conclusion

An ecological state is not simply whatever variables happen to be measured at one moment. It is a compression licensed to support specified scientific work. When that work consists of several responsibilities, the decisive object is the state satisfying them jointly.

The central quantitative lesson is

\[
\boxed{D_{\rm joint}\ne\sum_iD_i\quad\text{in general}.}
\]

More strongly, the positive discrepancy can be unbounded:

\[
\boxed{\Delta=\log_2n-1}
\]

in a finite sharp family, while zero individual debt for every responsibility forces zero joint debt. Conservation science therefore cannot assume that the monitoring resolution required for prediction, intervention, historical continuity, mechanism robustness, and evidence licensing can be budgeted independently. The distinctions one responsibility creates can be exactly those that activate another.

The same logic applies to change. Joint state burden can increase or decrease entirely through interaction even when every standalone responsibility debt remains fixed. CREST therefore reframes state design and state revision together: first determine what the full scientific responsibility requires of the world, then measure the joint obstruction structure and its changes, and only then ask what evidence is needed to identify that state. The practical question is no longer only how much each task costs in isolation, but how much information is created by having to satisfy them together—and how that information burden changes when the scientific contract changes.
