# CREST companion spine and publication decision — 2026-08-19

## Decision

Reject both of the following publication plans:

1. **four thin companion short reports, followed only later by CREST**; and
2. **an immediate broad-ecology CREST submission with no stable formal anchor**.

Adopt a **1 + 1 + 3 strategy**:

```text
CCOC full theorem paper + citable preprint/submission
  -> CREST full philosophy-of-ecology paper
  -> MLTR, MRM, and CED as independent focused full papers
```

A later general-ecology CREST paper is a separate optional project. It should be attempted only after a genuinely new cross-contract ecological result or application exists. It must not be a second presentation of the current J-series or a compressed summary of the four companion papers.

## Why the four companions are not short-report preliminaries

Each companion now owns a distinct scientific problem with enough formal structure for a full paper.

| Program | State-adequacy question | Core ecological principle | Primary remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| [CCOC](https://github.com/zuizui0223/ccoc) | Which distinctions become necessary when the legal future is opened? | Present functional equivalence need not imply open-future causal equivalence. | Retain additional future-relevant interface information or accept a lower bound on exact compression. |
| [MLTR](https://github.com/zuizui0223/mltr) | Does an inherited state meaning survive structural replacement? | Ecological categories are historically and structurally conditional. | Construct the unique coarsest source-relative exact repair and quantify transport defect. |
| [MRM](https://github.com/zuizui0223/mrm) | Do retained response mechanisms support one future prediction? | Visible-state equivalence need not imply mechanism-robust predictive equivalence. | Retain response-relevant distinctions, discriminate, or report typed/set-valued outcomes. |
| [CED](https://github.com/zuizui0223/ced) | Which distinctions does finite imperfect evidence justify reporting? | A model-required ecological distinction need not be an evidence-licensed report. | Preserve ambiguity, strengthen the evidence contract, or use risk-limited target resolution. |

The common root is not that all four use partitions or refinement. It is that a difference hidden by the present description can become scientifically relevant under a declared future, inherited semantics, retained mechanism family, or evidence target.

CREST therefore owns the upper question:

> **What counts as the same ecological state once the scientific commitments attached to that state are made explicit?**

Its central principle remains:

> **Ecological state identity is contract-relative, not intrinsic to the current visible configuration.**

This does not mean that nature is arbitrary or observer-created. It means that a scientific state variable is an equivalence claim, and its adequacy depends on the work the variable is required to perform.

## Why four short reports first is the wrong sequence

Waiting for four short publications would weaken rather than strengthen the program.

- It would encourage salami slicing of theorem families that already require full proofs, scope boundaries, and ecological interpretation.
- CREST would then look like a retrospective branding exercise rather than the program-forming account that explains why the four failures are scientifically different.
- Terminology and claim boundaries could drift across four independent review processes before the upper concept is fixed publicly.
- Repeated use of the same finite quotient/refinement substrate would make thin notes look redundant even when the quantified scientific questions differ.
- The strongest companion, CCOC, already provides enough of a formal anchor; publication of every companion is not a logical prerequisite for the CREST argument.

The companions should be called short reports only if a journal imposes a concise format and the proof remains complete. Concision is not itself the publication strategy.

## Why CREST should not be sent first as a general-ecology flagship

The current manuscript is a theorem-grounded **philosophy-of-ecology** paper. Its contribution is an account of state-representation adequacy, supported by four formal audits and explicit failure/remedy distinctions. That is already a substantial paper, but it is not yet the manuscript architecture expected for a top general-ecology Perspective.

A general-ecology version would need more than journal-name escalation. It would require:

1. one end-to-end ecological system in which all four contracts are instantiated on the same declared model;
2. at least one cross-contract result or failure that cannot be diagnosed by a companion axis alone;
3. named, ecologically testable predictions rather than only representational principles;
4. a quantitative worked model or analysis that shows when the proposed distinctions change ecological inference or management; and
5. a broad ecological claim that survives removal of the CREST vocabulary and repository history.

Until those gates are met, repackaging the current philosophy manuscript for a broad ecology journal would either overclaim or strip away the argument that presently makes it coherent.

## Journal decision

### Current CREST paper: Biology & Philosophy

**Biology & Philosophy is the primary target and should be treated as the good-journal objective for the current manuscript, not as a fallback.** Its scope directly includes conceptual, theoretical, and methodological issues in the biological sciences, and the present manuscript is already organized as an original philosophy-of-biology research article.

The submission should remain a full paper. The theorems serve as disciplined anchors, counterexamples, and boundaries; full proofs and executable witnesses remain in the repository and supplement.

### Fallback

**Philosophy, Theory, and Practice in Biology** remains the best-fit fallback if the paper is judged too programmatic or formally hybrid for Biology & Philosophy.

### General-ecology reach route

A future, distinct ecology-theory manuscript may be considered for:

- **Ecology Letters, Perspective**, after a successful proposal and only with broad ecological novelty, a quantitative model or analysis, and explicit testable predictions; or
- **The American Naturalist**, as a Major Article or a deliberately concise Inspiring Perspective, if the ecological theory itself—not only the philosophy of representation—becomes the main contribution.

This future paper must not duplicate the current CREST manuscript.

## Operational sequence

1. **CCOC:** finish and freeze the full theorem manuscript; post a stable citable preprint or place it under journal review.
2. **CREST:** finalize the current Biology & Philosophy manuscript while CCOC becomes citable. Do not wait for MLTR, MRM, and CED publication.
3. **MLTR, MRM, CED:** submit each as a focused full paper with its own headline result, proof spine, ecological interpretation, and claim firewall.
4. **Later synthesis:** pursue a general-ecology CREST paper only after a new coupled theorem or a real ecological worked application crosses the escalation gate above.

## CREST submission gate

CREST may proceed once:

- CCOC has a stable citable preprint or is under review;
- the philosophy manuscript is self-contained and does not imply that all companions are already published;
- every formal anchor is traceable to an analytic proof and executable witness;
- the four companion ownership boundaries remain explicit;
- generic fixed-point, quotient, refinement, repair, viability, and set-cover machinery is not presented as CREST novelty;
- all source, proof, interpretation, wording, authorship, funding, competing-interest, and AI-use disclosures have received final human review.

## One-sentence publication position

> **Publish one strong formal anchor, then publish CREST early as a full philosophy-of-ecology paper; do not delay it behind four thin notes, and do not target a broad ecology flagship until CREST has a genuinely ecological cross-contract result.**
