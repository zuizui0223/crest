# CREST versus predictive-state / POMDP formalisms — 2026-08-22

> **Decision:** CREST does not claim greater expressive power than a sufficiently augmented PSR/POMDP. The defensible distinction is architecture, typed scientific responsibility, and explicit separation of representational requirement from evidential licensing and target reportability.

## 1. What PSRs already do

Littman, Sutton & Singh's predictive representations of state represent controlled-system state by multi-step, action-conditional predictions of future observations. Singh, James & Rudary's PSR theory makes the point still more explicit: rows of the system-dynamics matrix index histories, columns index future tests, and entries are probabilities of those future tests given histories.

Therefore CREST must concede:

\[
\boxed{
\text{history + future action/observation predictions as state}
\text{ is established predictive-state theory.}
}
\]

The generic claim that a future experiment can make a present distinction state-relevant is not uniquely CREST.

## 2. What a sufficiently rich POMDP can encode

A POMDP can place in its hidden state any scientifically relevant latent variable: ecological history class, mechanism type, sediment legacy, interaction mode, or management context. Its action set can contain interventions; its observation kernel can represent imperfect monitoring; a reward/utility can represent a management target. Belief state then carries uncertainty over those hidden states.

Consequently, CREST should not claim:

- that its variables are inexpressible in POMDPs;
- that PSRs cannot encode future/intervention dependence;
- that belief-state methods cannot represent unresolved mechanism or history;
- that target-specific decision making requires CREST.

## 3. What CREST externalizes that generic predictive models usually leave model-internal

The distinction is methodological and philosophical rather than raw expressivity.

| Question | Generic augmented PSR/POMDP | CREST explicit object |
|---|---|---|
| Which futures are the state responsible for? | encoded by tests/action set/model design | future/composition contract; CCOC obstruction/lower bound |
| Which old category meaning must survive structural replacement? | can be encoded by augmented hidden state/reward constraints | inherited-semantic contract; MLTR source-relative portability/repair |
| Which mechanism alternatives matter? | latent model/state uncertainty | retained response types; MRM candidate-safe state / typed or set-valued report |
| Can the scientific obligations even share one admissible world domain? | normally assumed inside one model specification | explicit J3/J6 carrier existence/no-go gate |
| What is the least finite state satisfying the declared responsibilities? | model reduction / predictive-state minimization possible | J1 conditional least common adequate partition |
| Has observation identified that required state? | represented by belief/posterior | separate evidence partition / CED licensing gate |
| Can the requested target be reported without full state identification? | can be expressed through utility/QoI | explicit target-only reportability distinct from full-state licensing |
| What failed when the contract changes? | often seen as a changed model/task | typed future / semantic / mechanism / evidence failure and cross-gate certificates |

This table is the safe CREST distinction.

## 4. Embedding thought experiment

Take a finite CREST world

\[
u=(x,h,r,e),
\]

with present ecological configuration \(x\), inherited/history mode \(h\), response type \(r\), and evidence-relevant latent coordinates \(e\). A generic POMDP can use \(u\) as its hidden state. A PSR can, in principle, use a sufficiently rich set of future action-observation tests to represent the predictive consequences of histories generated by those hidden states.

This shows why **non-embeddability is not the CREST claim**.

CREST instead asks which coordinates may be erased under separately declared scientific responsibilities. It can then return different diagnoses:

- future grammar alone forces a split;
- inherited meaning forces a split even if a target-only model could relabel from scratch;
- mechanism disagreement forces ambiguity or a split;
- the required split exists but the evidence has not earned it;
- no common admissible carrier exists under the synchronized contract.

Those diagnoses can themselves be encoded in a larger generic model, but CREST makes them first-class scientific outputs rather than hidden modelling choices.

## 5. Cross-gate result that is stronger than the verbal comparison

CREST's strongest theorem-grounded distinction is not architecture alone. The finite action-expansion witness jointly realizes

\[
\text{viable carrier}\uparrow,
\qquad
\text{required state resolution}\uparrow,
\qquad
\text{full-state identifiability}\downarrow,
\]

while the declared target remains reportable.

O1 separately shows that the cheapest structural carrier repair and the cheapest fully evidence-licensed repair can differ.

These are concrete coupled patterns generated by the CREST decomposition. The manuscript may claim these finite patterns; it should not claim that no POMDP/PSR could reproduce them after suitable augmentation.

## 6. Snapshot sufficiency decision

The statement

\[
X(\omega)=X(\omega')
\Rightarrow
q_{\mathcal C,V}(\omega)=q_{\mathcal C,V}(\omega')
\]

is now classified as a **factorization criterion**, not a novelty-bearing theorem. It says exactly that the required state factors through the snapshot map \(X\).

Novel mathematics belongs in conditions that force or obstruct this factorization—for example CCOC future-addressability lower bounds, MLTR source-relative failure witnesses, MRM mechanism disagreement, or a future genuinely cross-contract theorem.

Do not create a named “Snapshot Sufficiency Theorem” from the implication alone.

## 7. Manuscript wording

### Safe

> Predictive state representations already show that controlled-system state can be represented by predictions of future action-conditioned observations. CREST does not claim a new predictive-state principle. Its contribution is an ecology-specific decomposition of state adequacy into future/composition, inherited-semantic, mechanism, carrier, evidence, and report responsibilities, together with finite cross-gate results that keep required state, identified state, and reportable target distinct.

### Unsafe

- “PSRs cannot represent CREST states.”
- “POMDPs cannot represent inherited history or mechanism uncertainty.”
- “CREST is more general than PSRs/POMDPs.”
- “Future interventions defining present state is novel.”
- “Snapshot sufficiency is a new theorem” when referring only to the factorization condition.

## 8. Development consequence

The comparison task is considered **closed by narrowing**, not by an irreducibility proof. A future stronger claim would require a theorem about a property that cannot be preserved by a stated class of PSR/POMDP representations under explicit restrictions. No such claim is needed for the current Biology & Philosophy paper.
