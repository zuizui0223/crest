# CREST ecological projection

> **Purpose:** project the proved finite CREST mathematics back into ecology without turning interpretations into unproved theorem families.

## 1. Ecological systems as self-modifying, partially observed dynamical worlds

CREST does not require ecosystems to be deterministic, mathematically chaotic, or globally fitness-maximizing.

The ecology-facing picture is instead

\[
\text{variation + stochasticity + interaction}
\quad+\quad
\text{context-dependent selection}
\]

inside a system in which organisms alter one another's environments and therefore one another's future selective and interaction conditions.

A useful schematic eco-evolutionary form is

\[
\dot x=F(x,\theta),
\qquad
\dot\theta=G(x,\theta),
\]

where ecological configuration \(x\) changes the selective context of traits or strategies \(\theta\), while changes in \(\theta\) alter ecological interactions and therefore the effective dynamics experienced by \(x\).

This motivates treating ecological worlds as **temporally extended and response-bearing**, rather than assuming that one present snapshot contains every state distinction required by science.

## 2. A state is a compression of a temporally extended world

Write a possible ecological world schematically as

\[
\omega=(h_t,x_t,\mathcal F_t),
\]

with relevant history \(h_t\), present configuration \(x_t\), and future-response structure \(\mathcal F_t\). For stochastic systems, \(\mathcal F_t\) may be a distribution over possible future paths rather than one predetermined future.

The central CREST interpretation is

\[
\boxed{
\text{ecological state}
=\text{ scientifically licensed compression of a temporally extended ecological world}.
}
\]

The state is not required to retain all of history or all possible futures. It retains only distinctions whose removal would invalidate the scientific work assigned to the state.

The current proved mathematics realizes this on declared finite latent-world carriers. A general continuous/stochastic trajectory theorem remains open.

## 3. Snapshot sufficiency

Let \(X(\omega)\) denote the present snapshot and \(q_{\mathcal C,V}(\omega)\) the adequate CREST state under observation/intervention context \(V\) and scientific contract \(\mathcal C\).

A present snapshot is sufficient exactly when

\[
\boxed{
X(\omega)=X(\omega')
\Rightarrow
q_{\mathcal C,V}(\omega)=q_{\mathcal C,V}(\omega').
}
\]

If the implication fails, two ecological worlds can look the same now yet require different states because of future accessibility, inherited meaning, latent mechanism, or another contract-relevant distinction.

CREST does not claim that snapshots are always insufficient. It turns snapshot sufficiency into an explicit condition to be checked.

## 4. Scientific access is a projection of the world

Science observes and intervenes through a context \(V\). Schematically,

\[
O_V:\Omega\to Y_V.
\]

Different measurement channels, scales, or intervention repertoires can induce different accessible distinctions while the underlying world remains unchanged.

This is not observer-relative truth. It is a distinction between:

1. the ecological world;
2. the distinctions a scientific setup can access; and
3. the distinctions a declared scientific contract requires.

CREST uses this separation to avoid treating one observed variable set as the ontology of the ecosystem.

## 5. Three structural reasons a present-state merge can fail

### CCOC — future insufficiency

CCOC contributes

\[
\boxed{
\text{current functional equivalence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{causal equivalence under an enlarged future}.
}
\]

A currently absent pollinator, corridor, colonizer, predator, pathogen reservoir, restoration option, or rewired interaction can expose a response distinction that the present state erased.

The important ecological variable is therefore not only the current interaction network but also the **space of interactions that can become relevant under the declared future grammar**.

### MLTR — historical / semantic insufficiency

MLTR asks whether an inherited ecological category remains exact after turnover or replacement.

The same current descriptor can carry different operational meaning after pollinator-guild replacement, species turnover, fragmentation, restoration, or alternative replacement histories.

\[
\boxed{
\text{same current descriptor}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{same inherited operational meaning}.
}
\]

History becomes state-relevant only when forgetting it changes a declared output, action, successor, or carried meaning.

### MRM — mechanistic insufficiency

MRM addresses hidden response mechanisms.

The same visible ecological state can be compatible with several causal explanations. CREST does not require full mechanism identity. Instead,

\[
\boxed{
\text{a latent mechanism difference becomes a state difference only when it changes a required response.}
}
\]

Distinct selective or causal mechanisms can generate the same observed ecological regularity under correlated environments, yet diverge under a decoupled environment or targeted intervention.

## 6. CED — evidence licensing after representational adequacy

CED is downstream of the structural question.

After the scientific contract requires a distinction, CED asks whether the observation and experiment architecture has actually resolved enough of that distinction to justify the requested claim.

Thus CREST separates:

\[
\boxed{
\text{required state}
\neq
\text{identified state}
\neq
\text{reportable target}
}
\]

in general.

Changing an observation channel does not change nature's underlying dynamics. It changes the quotient through which those dynamics are scientifically accessible and therefore what can be honestly reported.

## 7. Ecological rules as quotient laws

Let a scientific state partition merge worlds according to the distinctions judged irrelevant to a declared task. An effective rule is well-defined on that quotient only if worlds merged by the state agree on the response the rule must provide.

So some ecological regularities are best understood as

\[
\boxed{
\text{effective laws of an adequate quotient, not necessarily laws of the full latent world.}
}
\]

Changing scale, observation channel, future repertoire, structural history, or mechanism set can make an old quotient inadequate for a new question without making the original rule false in its original domain.

This gives a non-relativist explanation for why ecological regularities can be robust in one context and fail in another.

## 8. Context-dependent fitness and local adaptive direction

Natural selection need not imply a global evolutionary arrow toward one universal fitness maximum.

The ecology-facing interpretation is

\[
\boxed{
\text{stochastic variation}
+\text{ context-dependent biased filtering}.
}
\]

When the selective environment, competitor set, mutualist set, or frequency structure changes, the fitness ranking of the same variants can also change. A rule that ranks phenotypes in one ecological context can therefore fail in another because the response structure itself changed.

CREST uses this as motivation for context-sensitive state and rule adequacy; it does not currently prove a general eco-evolutionary fitness theorem.

## 9. Management can change the state before changing the ecosystem

The existing finite action-expansion result gives a sharp ecological interpretation.

A newly available management action can expose a response difference between worlds previously treated as equivalent. The action need not yet have been executed.

Thus:

> **An intervention does not merely act on an ecological state. By becoming scientifically relevant, it can change which ecological differences must count as state differences before it is used.**

This is a representational consequence of counterfactual responsibility, not backward causation from the future.

## 10. Dynamical, evolutionary, and representational stability

CREST keeps three questions separate.

### Dynamical stability

Does the ecological system resist perturbation, recover, remain within a basin, or preserve a relevant dynamical regime?

### Evolutionary stability

Does a strategy or trait resist invasion under the declared evolutionary model?

### Representational stability

Does the same state quotient remain adequate when the scientific context changes?

A system may be dynamically unchanged while its state representation becomes inadequate because new interventions are considered, new colonization routes become possible, a latent mechanism becomes decision-relevant, a new observation channel reveals a previously collapsed distinction, or an inherited category is moved into a structurally different system.

The existing action-expansion witness proves one strict case of this separation. A general theory connecting all three forms remains open.

## 11. Structural monitoring debt

For required finite state \(J\) and evidence partition \(E\), the minimum evidence refinement preserving existing evidence distinctions while identifying \(J\) is

\[
E\vee J.
\]

But the missing resolution need not be repayable by more replication of the same variable.

If two latent causal channels produce the same net output, repeated measurements of that net output can preserve the symmetry forever. A future intervention targeting one channel can nevertheless require the two worlds to be different states.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{monitoring adequacy may require a new measurement type, not merely more replication.}
}
\]

## 12. Program-level synthesis

The current CREST hierarchy is:

```text
self-modifying ecological world
        ↓
temporally extended possible worlds
        ↓
observation / intervention context
        ↓
snapshot sufficiency
        ↓
structural obstructions
    CCOC — future
    MLTR — history / inherited meaning
    MRM  — mechanism
        ↓
minimal adequate state
        ↓
evidence licensing
    CED
        ↓
quotient laws / reportability / representational stability
```

The ecology-facing claim is therefore:

> **Ecological sameness is a defeasible scientific compression of temporally extended worlds. It is constrained by the futures the system may enter, the meanings carried from its history, the mechanisms still capable of changing required responses, and the evidence through which those distinctions can be licensed.**

## 13. Research directions — not yet theorem claims

Priority directions are deliberately limited to work that strengthens this one spine:

1. finite-history or stochastic-response extension of the world-level formulation;
2. a sharper theory of representational stability under controlled contract changes;
3. observation-symmetry obstructions showing when a measurement family can never identify a required state;
4. ecological systems in which future, historical, mechanistic, and evidential distinctions can all be mapped to measurable quantities;
5. comparison with predictive-state, causal-state, effective-theory, and ecological stability literatures without claiming those generic ideas as new.

Do not create new named theorem families from ecological relabelings of existing refinement lemmas.
