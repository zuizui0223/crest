# Ecological necessity revision for Paper A

## Core biological thesis

The manuscript should not motivate the temporal cut as a monitoring device, a state-sufficiency audit, or a method for choosing variables. Its biological claim is prior to those applications:

> **Ecological state is not located entirely in the instantaneous observation.**

At one moment, two systems can share the same visible configuration while differing in distinctions retained from the past, embodied but hidden in present organization, or expressed in the futures that remain ecologically accessible. If any of those differences remain dynamically consequential, the two systems are not the same ecological state even though they occupy the same observational cut value.

The temporal cut is therefore needed to represent a biological fact about the ecological present: inherited past, latent present organization, and future-generating capacity meet at one boundary without being reducible to the same object.

## Ecological necessity chain

1. **Ecological memory and historical contingency** show that present dynamics can depend on antecedent conditions. The implication for state theory is not merely that history matters; it is that some past distinctions can remain constitutive of the present ecological state.
2. **Hysteresis, regime shifts, and restoration dynamics** show that similar current environmental or community configurations can support different recovery trajectories. Thus visible configuration and ecological state need not coincide.
3. **Latent organization** provides a second source of nonequivalence. Systems with the same coarse observation can differ in feedback structure, dormant propagules, physiological condition, interaction architecture, or other present response properties.
4. **Future accessibility** provides a third source. Two systems can look the same now yet differ in which ecological futures remain reachable: regeneration may still be possible in one but not the other, a response pathway may remain available in one but be lost in the other, or the same perturbation may expose different response classes.
5. These three sources are temporally asymmetric but meet at the present. A state concept that collapses any dynamically consequential distinction is biologically inadequate. CREST formalizes the least state on the cut that preserves the distinctions that genuinely constitute that ecological present.

## What the temporal cut is not

- not primarily a method for selecting measurements;
- not primarily a monitoring design;
- not a decision-support algorithm;
- not a claim that state is whatever an investigator chooses to ask;
- not a synonym for a latent variable in a fitted state-space model.

Those can be downstream applications. The paper itself is a theory of ecological state.

## Revised interpretation of the three directions

### Retrospective: retained past

The retrospective component represents distinctions whose causal origin lies before the cut but whose consequences remain embodied at the cut. Examples include nutrient legacy, drought memory, priority effects, disturbance history, and propagule or seed-bank legacies. CREST does not retain whole histories; it retains only historically distinct classes that remain ecologically nonequivalent at the present.

### Transverse: hidden present organization

The transverse component is not a second history variable. It represents distinctions that coexist at the same visible present but differ in current response organization. This can include feedback configuration, physiological or demographic composition, dormant stages, interaction structure, or other response-relevant latent organization. These differences are present now even if a coarse observation does not display them.

### Prospective: future-generating capacity

The prospective component should be framed biologically, not as researcher access. It represents distinctions in what futures remain ecologically expressible from the present organization under the declared class of perturbations or continuations. The future itself is not placed inside the present state; rather, present systems are distinguished when their remaining response possibilities differ.

## Shallow-lake biological witness

The shallow-lake section should cease to read primarily as a target-relative management exercise. Its biological role is to show why the instantaneous present can fail as state.

Two lakes can share the same coarse current water-quality description while differing in sediment phosphorus legacy and in present feedback/response configuration. Those differences can remain consequential even before any restoration decision is posed: they imply different recovery capacities and different responses to future change. The finite model is not evidence that real lakes instantiate its exact parity map. It is a compact witness that the ecological ingredients documented in shallow-lake restoration literature are sufficient to make one visible present compatible with several ecologically nonequivalent states.

The target-relative prerequisite examples can remain, but as secondary consequences: different ecological questions expose different parts of an already nontrivial present, rather than defining state by methodological fiat.

## Sparse semantic access: biological interpretation

Sparse access should not be sold primarily as incomplete experimental access. The ecological interpretation is that not every formally possible history-by-response combination supports the same future differentiation. Some combinations retain a response pathway; others have lost it or render it biologically meaningless. Therefore potential semantic combinations and realized future-generating distinctions need not coincide.

The quantity `k/N` is thus a property of how broadly prospective differentiation is supported across the ecological state space. The `log2(N/k)` result quantifies how strongly state complexity contracts when future-generating capacity is sparse across otherwise distinct retrospective-by-transverse combinations.

## One-sentence thesis

> **An ecological present is a temporal boundary at which retained history, hidden present organization, and future-generating capacity jointly determine which systems are genuinely the same state.**

## Strong opening question

> **When two ecosystems look the same now but differ in what they retain from the past, how they are organized beneath that appearance, or what futures remain possible, are they in the same ecological state?**

CREST answers no when those distinctions remain dynamically consequential, and defines the state at the temporal cut as the least equivalence structure that preserves them.
