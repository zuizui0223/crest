# CREST island–urban ecological validation registry — 2026-08-24

> Purpose: ground the current world → state → law → stability synthesis in real ecological systems without treating any empirical case as a proof of the finite CREST theorem.

## Validation logic

Each candidate system is scored against the same CREST questions:

1. **World:** what ecological history, present configuration, latent mechanism, and future-response structure plausibly belong to the possible-world description?
2. **Snapshot:** what present descriptor would a conventional analysis be tempted to call the state?
3. **Hidden distinction:** which history/mechanism/basin/interaction distinction can be invisible or collapsed by that snapshot?
4. **Future grammar:** which colonization, restoration, corridor, removal, introduction, or other intervention can expose the hidden distinction?
5. **Quotient law:** what coarse ecological rule is being assumed to hold on the proposed state quotient?
6. **CREST diagnosis:** snapshot insufficiency, future/composition insufficiency, inherited-semantic insufficiency, mechanism insufficiency, evidence insufficiency, law non-portability, or representational instability.
7. **Evidence grade:**
   - **A** — direct intervention, experimental manipulation, or a very close before/after counterfactual;
   - **B** — multi-site comparative/mechanistic evidence strongly constraining the CREST mapping;
   - **C** — plausible conceptual mapping that still lacks a discriminating empirical test.

The aim is not to force all CREST gates into every example. A good example is one that makes at least one distinction empirically unavoidable.

## Case I-A — Mauritius: interaction replacement is not functional-state replacement

### System

Mauritian island plant–frugivore communities, with a focused restoration example on Île aux Aigrettes.

### Empirical facts

A 2023 reconstruction of the Mauritian plant–frugivore network documented large losses of native interaction partnerships following extinction and a roughly comparable number of novel interactions involving introduced species. However, closer inspection of seed handling showed that many apparent replacements were ecologically different: interactions formerly involving seed dispersers were often replaced by seed predation. Thus binary interaction replacement did not imply recovery of seed-dispersal function.

A complementary in-situ intervention on Île aux Aigrettes introduced Aldabra giant tortoises as ecological substitutes for extinct giant tortoises. The endemic large-fruited ebony *Diospyros egrettarum* had been seed-dispersal limited after loss of native large-bodied frugivores. Introduced tortoises consumed and dispersed fruits; gut passage improved germination and new ebony seedlings became widely established.

### CREST mapping

- **Present snapshot / tempting state:** binary plant–frugivore network or total number of interaction links; alternatively, “frugivore interaction present”.
- **Hidden distinction:** seed disperser versus seed predator, seed handling, and the historical identity of the extinct/replacement interaction.
- **Future/intervention grammar:** introduction of a taxon substitute, loss or addition of a frugivore, restoration of a previously missing interaction.
- **Candidate coarse law:** “restored interaction count implies restored seed-dispersal function”.
- **Observed failure:** the coarse binary network can look repaired while the functional response remains different.

A suitable response map `R` therefore does not in general factor through a quotient that retains only binary interaction presence. The state must preserve at least those interaction distinctions that change the required functional response.

### CREST relevance

- mechanism insufficiency;
- inherited-semantic non-portability of the label “functional replacement”;
- quotient-law failure;
- evidence warning: interaction topology alone does not identify functional state;
- restoration action can change which distinctions must be retained.

### Evidence grade

**A.** The network reconstruction provides a direct functional mismatch, and the tortoise introduction provides an actual restoration intervention with a measured demographic/germination response.

### What this example does *not* prove

It does not prove the general CREST quotient theorem or the arbitrary-m capability–resolution theorem. It supplies a real ecological case in which a plausible coarse state (binary interaction replacement) demonstrably fails to preserve the response of interest.

### Core sources

- Griffiths et al. 2011, *Current Biology*, “Resurrecting Extinct Interactions with Extant Substitutes”, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2011.03.042.
- Heinen et al. 2023, *Nature Communications* 14:1019, “Novel plant–frugivore network on Mauritius is unlikely to compensate for the extinction of seed dispersers”, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-36669-9.

## Case I-B — Izu Islands *Campanula*: same island label, different pollination and breeding-state history

### System

*Campanula punctata* / the Izu-island lineage historically studied across mainland Honshu and the Izu Islands.

### Empirical facts

Inoue and Amano (1986) found strong changes in pollinator assemblage across mainland and islands: *Bombus diversus* was predominant on Honshu, *Bombus ardens* plus halictid bees on Oshima, and halictid bees on Niijima, Kozushima, and Hachijo. Island flowers were smaller than mainland flowers. Breeding-system experiments showed mainland plants were mostly highly self-incompatible, Oshima was mixed, and Hachijo plants were self-compatible and potentially autogamous.

Inoue (1988), extending bagging experiments to six Izu Islands, found most Honshu and Oshima plants self-incompatible while almost all plants on the other Izu Islands were self-compatible. Bumblebees were absent from the Izu Islands except Oshima; the dominant pollinators elsewhere were halictid bees. The results supported a bumblebee-absence / reduced-pollination-efficiency hypothesis for breeding-system evolution.

### CREST mapping

- **Present snapshot / tempting state:** “island population” or even a present floral phenotype.
- **Hidden/history distinction:** pollinator-loss history, breeding-system history, and current latent capacity for autonomous selfing.
- **Future grammar:** arrival/reintroduction of a bumblebee, altered pollinator access, changed connectivity among islands, or experimental pollinator addition/removal.
- **Candidate coarse law:** “island status determines reproductive state” or “current floral form is sufficient to predict pollination response”.
- **Observed warning:** Oshima is an explicit counterexample to a simple island/non-island partition because its bumblebee context and breeding system differ from the other islands.

### CREST relevance

- historical/semantic insufficiency;
- local adaptive direction without a universal island law;
- prospective future/composition test: if a pollinator becomes newly available, previously irrelevant distinctions in compatibility/flower traits can become response-relevant;
- a natural candidate for testing whether present phenotype alone factors the required reproductive-state quotient.

### Evidence grade

**B** for the historical comparative claim; **C/A prospective** for a direct CREST intervention test, depending on whether current field experiments manipulate or observe restored/new pollinator access.

### High-value next measurement

For a modern CREST test, pair present floral/breeding descriptors with intervention-sensitive response measurements: pollinator availability, stigma contact/pollen deposition, bagging/open pollination, and a declared future action such as access by a large bee guild. The discriminating question is whether two populations that look similar now diverge under the same added interaction opportunity.

### Core sources

- Inoue & Amano 1986, *Plant Species Biology* 1:89–97, doi:10.1111/j.1442-1984.1986.tb00018.x.
- Inoue 1988, *Plant Species Biology* 3:125–128, doi:10.1111/j.1442-1984.1988.tb00178.x.

## Case U-A — White clover across 160 cities: “urban” is not one portable fitness law

### System

The Global Urban Evolution Project (GLUE) white-clover (*Trifolium repens*) cyanogenesis system.

### Empirical facts

Santangelo et al. (2022) sampled 110,019 plants from 6,169 populations across 160 cities in 26 countries. Urban–rural gradients were associated with significant cyanogenesis clines in 47% of cities. The strength of clines varied among cities and was explained in part by city-specific changes in drought stress and vegetation cover. Genomic data from 2,074 plants across 26 cities supported adaptive evolution, but the degree of parallel adaptation differed strongly among cities.

Earlier work in Toronto, New York, Boston, and Montreal found urban declines in cyanogenesis in three of four cities but no cline in Montreal. The mechanistic interpretation implicated winter ground temperature and snow cover rather than a universal “urban heat” rule. A 2026 nine-city Midwestern study further reported no consistent regional relationship between cyanogenesis and the tested environmental predictors, with heterogeneous within-city associations.

### CREST mapping

- **Present snapshot / tempting state:** distance to city centre, impervious surface, or a binary “urban” label.
- **Hidden/context distinction:** drought, vegetation, snow cover, freezing regime, local herbivory, genotype frequencies, and the city-specific covariance structure among these variables.
- **Future-response structure:** fitness consequences of cyanogenesis under the local stress combination.
- **Candidate coarse law:** “urbanization causes cyanogenesis to decline” or any single universal urban fitness ranking.
- **Observed failure:** the sign/strength/existence of the cline is not portable across all cities.

The same categorical urban label therefore does not define one context-free evolutionary state. A more adequate quotient must preserve the environmental distinctions on which the response law depends.

### CREST relevance

- quotient-law non-portability;
- representational instability across cities;
- eco-evolutionary self-modification / local adaptive bias rather than global teleology;
- strong example that the same nominal state label (“urban”) can support different effective laws because it erases different environmental distinctions in different cities.

### Evidence grade

**A/B.** Exceptionally strong replicated comparative and genomic evidence for context-dependent adaptive response; not an intervention on the city itself, but strong enough to reject a simple universal urban-state law.

### Direct CREST test using available data

Fit two models across cities:

1. a quotient using only an urbanization coordinate;
2. a richer quotient adding environmental dimensions such as drought/vegetation/snow or the published environmental PCs.

Then test portability by leave-one-city-out prediction. CREST predicts that the richer quotient should reduce systematic cross-city law failure where the omitted context variables are response-relevant. This is a falsifiable empirical projection of the quotient-law story, not a proof of CREST.

### Core sources

- Santangelo et al. 2022, *Science* 375:1275–1281, doi:10.1126/science.abk0989.
- Thompson et al. 2016/2017, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*, “Urbanization drives the evolution of parallel clines in plant populations”.
- 2026 *Evolutionary Ecology* study: “Cyanogenesis variation in white clover is decoupled from regional climate and human footprint across a longitudinal gradient”, doi:10.1007/s10682-026-10400-8.

## Case U-B — Urban pollinator connectivity: apparent patch isolation is not causal closure

### System

Urban green-space pollination/connectivity, with three complementary data types.

### Empirical facts

A 2024 pollen-tracking study followed fluorescent quantum-dot pollen movement among four isolated patches of urban greenery. Pollen transfer occurred frequently among small isolated flowering patches even in the absence of green corridors; streets, trails, and pavements did not prevent pollen movement. The determinants differed among focal plant species.

A 2026 field experiment in Southwest England constructed six 30-m artificial linear features in urban and rural meadows and placed seven-species phytometer assemblages at their ends. Linked assemblages had higher overall seed set; three bee-pollinated species benefited significantly, whereas fly- and moth-pollinated species were less affected.

A 2026 Padua study sampled bees and hoverflies at 105 sites across six urban habitat types. The city-scale species–habitat network was generally robust, but robustness declined when the highest-quality patches were removed first; green area in the landscape benefited wild bees, while local floral cover and low mowing were especially important.

### CREST mapping

- **Present snapshot / tempting state:** geometrically isolated patch, patch type, or local species richness.
- **Hidden distinction:** realized pollinator movement, guild identity, stepping-stone structure, and dependence on a small set of high-quality resource patches.
- **Future grammar:** open/close a corridor, add/remove high-quality patches, modify mowing/flower resources, or connect previously separated assemblages.
- **Candidate coarse law:** “physically isolated urban patches are functionally closed” or “a corridor has one common pollination effect”.
- **Observed failure:** pollen can cross apparently isolated urban space, and the functional effect of a new connection is pollinator-guild dependent.

### CREST relevance

- direct challenge to identifying causal closure from map geometry alone;
- future/composition insufficiency: adding a connection changes the response repertoire;
- mechanism/guild insufficiency: the same corridor intervention has different effects for bee-, fly-, and moth-pollinated plants;
- evidence insufficiency: local patch observations or Euclidean isolation alone do not identify the functional connectivity state.

### Evidence grade

**A** for the corridor manipulation; **B** for the pollen-tracking and city-network structure.

### Core sources

- 2024 *Acta Oecologica* 123:103985, “Pollinator-mediated connectivity in fragmented urban green spaces—tracking pollen grain movements in the city center”, doi:10.1016/j.actao.2024.103985.
- Li, Clements & Memmott 2026, *Oecologia* 208:64, “Linear features affect pollination success in experimental plant assemblages”, doi:10.1007/s00442-026-05899-1.
- Geppert et al. 2026, *Biological Conservation* 315:111680, “Species-habitat networks inform pollinator conservation strategies in cities”, doi:10.1016/j.biocon.2025.111680; data archived on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.18059923).

# Matched island–urban comparison

| CREST question | Island systems | Urban systems |
|---|---|---|
| What does a snapshot erase? | extinct interaction history, pollinator-loss history, interaction function | local environmental covariance, realized movement, guild-specific connectivity |
| What opens the future? | taxon substitution, recolonization, new mutualist/pollinator | corridor construction, patch management, urban greening/redevelopment |
| What coarse rule fails? | “interaction replaced = function restored”; “island = one reproductive state” | “urban = one fitness law”; “isolated patch = functionally closed” |
| What becomes state-relevant? | seed handling, replacement identity, breeding system, pollinator guild | drought/snow/vegetation context, pollinator guild, stepping stones, key patches |
| Stability contrast | present community can look restored while functional state differs | city/patch can be physically unchanged while a planning intervention changes the required state |

The matched prediction is not “islands and cities are the same.” It is:

> **Different fragmentation and replacement mechanisms can generate the same representational failure: a coarse present label remains descriptively usable while ceasing to preserve the response required by a new future, mechanism, or reporting contract.**

2. **Global urban white clover** — to show that a nominally shared environmental state (“urban”) does not carry one portable adaptive law.

# Falsifiable CREST projections

1. **Functional-network projection:** a quotient retaining interaction function/handling type should predict restoration outcomes better across Mauritius-like replacement systems than binary interaction count alone.
2. **Urban-law portability projection:** a richer environmental quotient should outperform a city-centre-distance/urban-only quotient in leave-one-city-out prediction of white-clover cyanogenesis clines.
3. **Closure projection:** geometric isolation should be a weaker predictor of pollen exchange than a quotient that includes realized/possible pollinator movement and stepping-stone resources.
4. **Izu future-response projection:** populations with similar present floral descriptors but different compatibility/pollinator context should diverge under the same newly available large-pollinator interaction if the snapshot is not sufficient.

These are empirical projections of CREST. Failure of these projections would weaken the ecological usefulness of the proposed quotient choices even though it would not refute the finite mathematical theorem.
