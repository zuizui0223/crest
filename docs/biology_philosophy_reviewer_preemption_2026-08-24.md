# CREST Biology & Philosophy reviewer-preemption audit — 2026-08-24

## Purpose

Stress-test the blinded CREST submission against the most likely reviewer objections before journal submission. This document does not add a theorem family and does not expand the empirical scope.

## Reviewer challenge 1 — “This is just purpose-relative adequacy.”

### Risk

High. Adequacy-for-purpose is established philosophy of modelling and is now explicitly advocated in environmental sciences. CREST must not claim novelty for the proposition that representations, datasets, or models are adequate only relative to aims and context.

### Required answer

CREST's novelty claim is narrower:

1. ecological worlds are separated from the state required by a scientific responsibility;
2. required state is separated from the state actually identified by evidence;
3. both are separated from the reportable target;
4. quotient-level law validity is tied to the adequacy of the state fibers;
5. within this architecture, one connected finite construction proves a cross-gate no-bound result:

\[
\Delta |K^*|=1,
\qquad
\Delta K_{U_0}=m
\]

for arbitrary finite \(m\), while fixed evidence loses full-state licensing and a coarse target can remain reportable.

The manuscript must say explicitly that **purpose-relativity itself is not the novelty**.

## Reviewer challenge 2 — “Why is this ecology rather than generic controlled-system theory?”

### Risk

High.

### Required answer

The finite proof is intentionally generic enough to be mathematically clean. The ecology-specific contribution is the explanatory target and typed decomposition: ecological state identity under future/composition, inherited ecological meaning/history, retained mechanism-response alternatives, admissible world/carrier constraints, evidence licensing, and report targets.

Do not claim that the underlying automata/partition mathematics is uniquely ecological. Instead claim that CREST gives an ecology-specific theory of **state sameness** and uses the finite theorem to show a limitation that directly matters for ecological generalization and management-relevant state definitions.

A safe sentence is:

> The proof is not ecology-specific mathematics; the contribution is an ecology-specific state-adequacy architecture whose layers make the cross-gate separation scientifically interpretable.

## Reviewer challenge 3 — “Predictive State Representations / causal states already define state by future tests.”

### Risk

High.

### Required answer

Concede fully that predictive equivalence and action-conditional future tests are prior art. CREST must not claim to invent future-defined state.

The defended difference is that CREST keeps separately inspectable:

- future/composition sufficiency;
- inherited-semantic portability;
- mechanism-response robustness;
- carrier feasibility;
- evidence identification;
- target reportability;
- quotient-law validity.

The key theorem is not “future actions can change state abstraction.” It is the quantitative cross-gate separation with unit carrier gain and arbitrary state/evidence-resolution growth.

## Reviewer challenge 4 — “State/action abstraction coupling is already known.”

### Risk

High.

### Required answer

Concede Konidaris-style state/action coupling. Block any wording implying that CREST discovered that adding actions can require a different state abstraction.

The theorem-level claim is instead:

> a single additional action can add exactly one viable world while forcing arbitrarily many additional bits of least-state resolution on a retained present slice, with simultaneous full-state licensing loss under fixed evidence and retained coarse-target reportability.

The no-bound consequence, not qualitative state/action coupling, carries novelty.

## Reviewer challenge 5 — “The binary-address witness is artificial.”

### Risk

Medium-high.

### Required answer

Accept that the witness is deliberately extremal. Its role is a **counterexample to a universal upper bound**, not a model of typical ecosystem scaling.

The conclusion is therefore conditional and negative:

> without additional structural assumptions, small viability/capability gain alone cannot guarantee small representational burden.

Do not infer exponential growth in real ecosystems. Ecological structure may yield much tighter bounds; identifying such sufficient conditions is future work.

This framing converts “artificial witness” from a weakness into the standard role of a sharp impossibility construction.

## Reviewer challenge 6 — “Why temporally extended worlds? Isn't a current Markov state enough?”

### Risk

Medium.

### Required answer

CREST does not deny that a current Markov state can be sufficient. Snapshot sufficiency is explicitly allowed and is characterized by factorization through the present-snapshot map.

Temporally extended worlds are used because ecology often has candidate state descriptions that are not known a priori to be Markov-sufficient. History, latent mechanism, or counterfactual response may separate worlds with the same observed present descriptor.

The claim is not “ecological state must always contain history.” It is:

> history or latent response structure requires distinct state only when it changes a response, inherited meaning, or target required by the declared task.

## Reviewer challenge 7 — “Contract-relativity is arbitrary or observer-relative truth.”

### Risk

Medium.

### Required answer

Separate scientific responsibility from ecological constraint. Scientists declare which predictions/actions/targets the representation must support, but they cannot arbitrarily decide that two worlds are equivalent if the worlds disagree on a required response.

Thus contract-relativity is closer to task-relative adequacy than relativism. The full-world response structure constrains admissible quotients.

Use **domain-relative law validity**, never “observer-relative truth.”

## Reviewer challenge 8 — “Why include evidence in the state theory?”

### Risk

Medium.

### Required answer

Evidence is not a fourth ontic ingredient of ecological state. CED is downstream. CREST distinguishes:

\[
\text{required state}\neq\text{identified state}\neq\text{reportable target}.
\]

The evidence layer prevents a common inferential error: deriving a theoretically necessary distinction and then speaking as though it has been empirically identified.

This separation is particularly relevant in ecology because monitoring schemes often target proxies or management outcomes rather than full latent system state.

## Reviewer challenge 9 — “What does conservation actually gain?”

### Risk

Medium-high for ecology-facing reviewers.

### Required answer

Do not sell a new conservation protocol. The defensible implications are conceptual constraints:

1. **Present similarity does not by itself establish management-relevant state equivalence.**
2. **Functional redundancy is future-contract relative:** systems equivalent for current function can differ under disturbance, reconnection, turnover, or intervention.
3. **Target achievement is not full-state identification.** A restoration or conservation target may be reportable while relevant latent state remains unresolved.
4. **More replication of the same observation channel may not repair a structural state-identification deficit.** A qualitatively different measurement may be required.
5. **When the management repertoire changes, the adequacy of the state variables should be re-audited.**

These are theoretical projections, not claims of empirical validation.

## Reviewer challenge 10 — “The framework has too many moving parts.”

Keep the main manuscript hierarchy minimal:

CCOC, MLTR, MRM, and CED should appear as explanatory roles, not as four separate research programmes the reader must learn in full.

## Reviewer challenge 11 — “Is the least-state theorem itself novel?”

### Risk

Medium.

### Required answer

No. The manuscript should explicitly state that closure operators, partition lattices, fixed-point refinement, automata minimization, and related machinery are classical. J1 is the finite existence/minimality backbone, not the headline novelty.

The headline is the connected capability-resolution separation and no-bound consequence.

## Reviewer challenge 12 — “Where exactly is the novelty?”

### Required two-sentence answer

> CREST does not claim novelty for predictive equivalence, purpose-relative adequacy, hidden-state modelling, state/action abstraction coupling, or ecological state-and-transition thinking. Its contribution is to separate ecological-world feasibility, task-required state, evidence-identified state, reportable target, and quotient-law validity within one state-sameness architecture, and to prove in one connected finite construction that a unit capability/carrier gain can force arbitrarily large growth in required state and monitoring resolution while preserving a coarser report target.

## Submission claim ceiling

### Safe

- “We provide an ecology-specific state-adequacy architecture.”
- “We prove a connected finite no-bound separation across carrier, state, evidence, and target layers.”
- “The result shows that capability gain alone is insufficient to bound representational burden without further structural assumptions.”
- “The theory yields conservation-relevant constraints on when present similarity, target achievement, or repeated monitoring licenses claims about ecological state.”

### Blocked

- “CREST is the first purpose-relative theory of ecological state.”
- “CREST is the first framework in which actions determine state abstraction.”
- “CREST discovers that ecological history matters.”
- “CREST proves real ecosystems have exponentially growing state complexity.”
- “CREST provides a validated conservation monitoring protocol.”
- “CREST is more expressive than POMDPs or PSRs.”

## Decision

The manuscript is defensible for submission if the final blinded candidate preserves these concessions and keeps the novelty concentrated on the typed ecological architecture plus the connected no-bound theorem. No additional theorem or empirical section is required for reviewer defense.
