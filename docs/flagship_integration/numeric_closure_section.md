# Numeric closure of the joint-debt result

The main theorem is asymptotic, but the paper should end with concrete finite numbers rather than only with the statement that \(\Delta\) is unbounded.

For the marked-cycle sharp family, take the baseline partition to have one class. Audit 1 alone creates one bit of state debt, so its standalone refinement has two classes:

\[
D_1=1,\qquad D_2=0.
\]

The common fixed point is discrete on \(n\) worlds, hence

\[
D_{\rm joint}=\log_2 n,
\qquad
\Delta=\log_2 n-1.
\]

Because the standalone accounting predicts two required classes while the joint contract requires \(n\), the state-count underestimation factor is

\[
\boxed{U_{\#}(n)=\frac{n}{2}.}
\]

The debt underestimation factor is

\[
\boxed{U_K(n)=\frac{D_{\rm joint}}{D_1+D_2}=\log_2 n.}
\]

The fraction of realized joint debt that is interaction-generated is

\[
\boxed{F_{\rm int}(n)=\frac{\Delta}{D_{\rm joint}}
=1-\frac{1}{\log_2 n}.}
\]

Thus three different statements are simultaneously true in the same sharp family:

1. the absolute missing debt \(\Delta\) diverges;
2. the number of required state classes missed by standalone accounting grows as \(n/2\); and
3. the interaction-generated share of total debt tends to one.

The \(n=1024\) row is a useful manuscript anchor. Responsibility-wise accounting says that the system needs only two state classes, whereas the joint contract requires 1024. The class-count underestimate is therefore 512-fold. In information units the independent estimate is 1 bit, the true joint burden is 10 bits, and 9 of those 10 bits—90% of the realized joint burden—are interaction-generated.

This is stronger and more interpretable than saying only that \(\Delta\to\infty\). It states exactly how a modular audit can fail at a finite scale.

## A fully resolved three-obstruction finite example

The six-world CCOC/MLTR/MRM cascade provides a second, non-asymptotic closure in which all three named structural responsibilities appear on one common carrier.

The baseline has two classes. The exact coalition table is

| responsibility coalition | required classes | debt from baseline (bit) |
|---|---:|---:|
| none | 2 | 0.000000 |
| CCOC | 3 | 0.584963 |
| MLTR | 2 | 0.000000 |
| MRM | 2 | 0.000000 |
| CCOC + MLTR | 4 | 1.000000 |
| CCOC + MRM | 3 | 0.584963 |
| MLTR + MRM | 2 | 0.000000 |
| CCOC + MLTR + MRM | 5 | 1.321928 |

Therefore

\[
D_{\rm joint}=1.3219280949\ \text{bit},
\qquad
\Delta=0.7369655942\ \text{bit}.
\]

The standalone picture predicts a 2-to-3-state refinement, whereas the full contract requires 2-to-5 states. Joint representation therefore needs

\[
\boxed{\frac{5}{3}=1.6667}
\]

times as many state classes as standalone accounting predicts.

The exact interaction anatomy is

\[
0.7369655942
=
0.4150374993
+
0.3219280949,
\]

where the first term is the CCOC×MLTR pairwise dividend and the second is the genuinely three-way CCOC×MLTR×MRM dividend. Hence 56.3% of the interaction-generated burden is pairwise and 43.7% is three-way.

The order-independent Shapley attribution of total joint debt is

| obstruction | Shapley contribution (bit) | share of joint debt |
|---|---:|---:|
| CCOC | 0.899791 | 68.07% |
| MLTR | 0.314828 | 23.82% |
| MRM | 0.107309 | 8.12% |

MLTR and MRM each have zero standalone debt but nonzero joint attribution. They are not weak obstructions in this example; they are dormant obstructions activated by distinctions introduced elsewhere in the contract.

## Manuscript closing statement

The numerical endpoint should therefore be stated in both asymptotic and finite form:

> In the sharp family, a one-bit standalone audit can conceal a joint requirement of \(\log_2 n\) bits and \(n\) state classes. At \(n=1024\), independent accounting predicts 2 classes while the common fixed point requires 1024: a 512-fold class-count underestimate, with 90% of the realized joint debt generated by interaction. In the explicit three-obstruction six-world system, the corresponding finite mismatch is 3 predicted classes versus 5 jointly required, with 0.736966 interaction bits split into 0.415037 pairwise and 0.321928 genuinely three-way debt.

This closes the mathematical paper numerically without appealing to empirical data.
