# CREST v0.7 literature-positioning revision

Date: 2026-09-09

This revision changes scientific communication only. It does not alter the sparse semantic-access theorem, semantic quotient implementation, XOR shallow-lake witness, or canonical numeric benchmark.

## Major Article decision

The flagship remains targeted as a **Major Article**, not a Note. The reason is conceptual scope: the manuscript proposes a general modeling architecture and needs room to distinguish itself from neighboring ecological and state-representation traditions. The current inclusive main-text count remains far below the journal's usual 7,500-word ceiling.

## Word-count policy

The prior submission helper removed display/inline mathematics and markdown table rows before counting. That custom exclusion rule is dropped. The reported text count now uses the complete Introduction-through-Conclusion source without subtracting mathematics or table rows; only Literature Cited lies outside the main-text interval.

## Section 10 expansion

Section 10 now distinguishes four neighboring traditions:

1. **Ecological memory and historical contingency** — Ogle et al. (2015) and Fukami (2015) establish that antecedent conditions and assembly order matter; CREST asks which retrospective distinctions survive state compression.
2. **Hysteresis, alternative states, and restoration** — Scheffer et al. (2001), Beisner et al. (2003), and Suding et al. (2004) motivate separating current configuration from dynamical history/feedback structure; CREST does not infer basins or claim alternative stable states.
3. **Ecological state-space models** — Auger-Méthé et al. (2021) estimate latent states under specified process/observation models; CREST treats the choice of which distinctions belong in the retained state as an upstream identification problem.
4. **Predictive states and state abstraction** — Shalizi and Crutchfield (2001), Littman et al. (2001), Givan et al. (2003), and Li et al. (2006) provide the quotient/equivalence substrate; CREST keeps retrospective semantics, latent-response equivalence, and future-query legality separate before composition and adds explicit semantic-access coverage.

## Claim boundary

The revision does not claim that CREST supersedes these literatures or that quotient states are novel. It positions CREST as an ecological identification discipline connecting them while preserving their different scientific roles.
